## Introduction / Background Notes

- Hearing loss is common, under-diagnosed, and associated with social isolation, cognitive decline, and reduced quality of life.
- Disparities by race, socioeconomic status, and rural residence are documented nationally; Mississippi-specific clinical data are sparse.
- UMMC serves a large, diverse, and largely rural catchment; the RDW offers a practical window into real-world diagnosis and care patterns.
- CDC PLACES provides county-level model-based estimates of hearing disability that can situate UMMC findings in statewide context.

---

## Methods Outline

**Data sources**
1. UMMC Patient Cohort Explorer / Research Data Warehouse (de-identified Epic data).
2. CDC PLACES county-level hearing disability estimates.
3. County Health Rankings (primary-care access, uninsured, rurality) for contextual analysis.

**Cohort definition (to refine)**
- Inclusion: Patients with ≥1 encounter carrying an ICD-10 diagnosis code for hearing loss (sensorineural, conductive, mixed, or unspecified) in the available RDW window.
- Key variables: age, sex, race/ethnicity, (ZIP/county if present), encounter type, presence of audiology or otolaryngology specialty encounters, relevant procedures.

**Analysis**
- Descriptive statistics overall and stratified by race and rural vs non-rural residence.
- Simple proportions for specialty follow-up.
- Optional: comparison of UMMC hearing-loss documentation rates against PLACES county estimates for the surrounding region.

## Discussion Notes (early)

- Strengths: large real-world academic medical center cohort; ability to examine both diagnosis and care utilization; linkage to statewide county estimates.
- Limitations: single-center, de-identified data (possible residual confounding, limited social determinants), diagnosis codes may under-capture true prevalence, date-shifting, etc.
- Implications: opportunities for improved screening, referral pathways, and outreach in high-disparity counties or demographic groups.
